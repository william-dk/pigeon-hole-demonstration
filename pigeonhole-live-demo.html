A question about repeated differences

For a set S of m distinct integers chosen from 1 through n, each unordered pair contributes a positive difference. There are m(m − 1)/2 pairs and only n − 1 possible differences.

The counting guarantee
If m(m − 1)/2 > (r − 1)(n − 1), at least r distinct pairs share one difference. Distinct pairs may share an endpoint.

Observed repetitions describe the selected set. The counting inequality gives a statement about every set of that size. When the inequality fails, counting alone is inconclusive. In particular, for n = 16, six selected numbers still force a repetition by a stronger argument.
